Focus mode vs an app blocker on iPhone

People search for a "focus app" and find two different things with the same name. One keeps the phone from interrupting you. The other keeps you from interrupting yourself.

Updated 18 September 2026

What an iPhone Focus does

Focus lives in Settings → Focus and in Control Center. Do Not Disturb, Work, Sleep and Personal are all Focus modes, and you can make your own. When one is on, it can:

  • Silence notifications, except from the people and apps you allow.
  • Hide notification badges and dim the Lock Screen.
  • Show only chosen Home Screen pages, so the distracting apps aren't in front of you.
  • Apply Focus filters inside some apps, such as showing one mailbox or one calendar.
  • Turn on by schedule, location, or when you open a certain app.

It's free, it's already on your phone, and for incoming distraction it is very good.

What a Focus doesn't do

A Focus does not block apps. With Work Focus on and the Home Screen page hidden, Instagram is still a swipe away in the App Library or Spotlight, and it opens normally. Focus manages what reaches you. It has no opinion about what you reach for.

That matters because a lot of phone use isn't triggered by a notification. It's the automatic pick-up: a dull moment, a hard paragraph, a lift ride. Silence doesn't help with that.

What an app blocker does

An app blocker works on the other side. It uses Apple's Screen Time system to put a block screen over the apps you choose. Tap the icon and the app doesn't open. iOS has a basic version built in (App Limits and Downtime, covered in how to block apps on iPhone), though those come with an Ignore Limit button unless you set a passcode.

Clam is a blocker reduced to one action. Pick your apps once. Tap Clam up, choose a length from 5 minutes to 4 hours, lock your phone. The countdown shows on the Lock Screen. A blocked app shows "clammed up" and the minutes left. Ending early takes a 10-second hold and costs your streak.

Side by side

  • Stops notifications: Focus yes. App blocker no.
  • Stops you opening an app: Focus no. App blocker yes.
  • Runs on a schedule: Focus yes. Downtime yes. Clam no; it's for sessions you start.
  • Cost: Focus and the built-in Screen Time limits are free. Clam is a paid app with a free trial.

Use both

They don't compete. A setup that works for a block of deep work:

  1. Turn on a Work Focus that allows calls from the few people who might really need you.
  2. Start a blocker session on the apps you reach for out of habit.
  3. Put the phone face down, out of arm's reach if you can.

The Focus handles what comes in. The blocker handles the reflex. Neither fixes a task you don't want to do, but together they remove the two easiest exits.

If you only pick one

Look at your Screen Time report in Settings. If the notifications count is high and pick-ups follow notifications, start with a Focus. If most pick-ups go straight to the same two or three apps with nothing prompting them, a Focus won't change much, and blocking those apps will.
